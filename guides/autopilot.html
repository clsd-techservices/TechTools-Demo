Autopilot Import & Check

Get devices into Windows Autopilot with the right group tag, and check whether any device is ready for a clean OOBE.

1 · Collect hardware hashes

On each device (admin PowerShell, or Shift+F10 during OOBE):

Set-ExecutionPolicy -Scope Process RemoteSigned -Force
Install-Script -Name Get-WindowsAutopilotInfo -Force
Get-WindowsAutopilotInfo -OutputFile C:\HWID\hashes.csv

Combine files from many devices into one CSV with a single header row. Vendors and resellers can also supply hashes when you order.

2 · Upload and check

Every row is checked before import. Rows are skipped, with the reason shown, when the hash is missing, truncated, or not valid base64, the serial is missing, or the serial or hash appears twice.

Offline? Use Download clean CSV and upload it in Intune: Devices › Enrollment › Windows Autopilot › Devices › Import.

3 · Group tags

A tag set at import puts devices straight into the right profile. Match it with a dynamic device group:

(device.devicePhysicalIds -any (_ -eq "[OrderID]:{{groupTagFirst}}"))

Replace the tag with your own, one group per tag.

Import errors

ErrorWhat to do
ZtdDeviceAlreadyAssignedAlready in your tenant. Nothing to do, or delete the old record to re-import.
ZtdDeviceAssignedToOtherTenantOwned by another organization. Ask the previous owner or reseller to deregister it.
ZtdDeviceBadHardwareHashDamaged hash. Re-run Get-WindowsAutopilotInfo on the device.
Throttled (429)The tool retries automatically. If it still fails, wait a minute and import again.
Pending a long timeNormal for busy tenants. Allow up to 15 minutes, then use Refresh status.

Good to know

  • Profile assignment usually follows import within 5–15 minutes. A just-imported device scores lower until it does.
  • The sample CSVs in samples/ use made-up hashes. Use them to see the checks and demo mode; Intune will reject them.
  • Needs the DeviceManagementServiceConfig.ReadWrite.All and DeviceManagementManagedDevices.Read.All permissions.
